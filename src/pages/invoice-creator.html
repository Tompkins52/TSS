What belongs on an invoice

The things an accounts payable clerk looks for, in the order they look for them. Leave one out and the invoice goes to the bottom of the pile.

  1. Who you are. Legal business name, address, phone, and email. Add your tax ID when a customer asks for it; it should match the W-9 you gave them.
  2. The word "Invoice" and a number nobody else has. Sequential, never reused. The tool remembers your next number.
  3. Three dates. The invoice date, the due date, and the terms that produced it (Net 30 means thirty days from the invoice date). "Due on receipt" is a term too.
  4. Who pays. The customer's name, billing address, an attention line, and their purchase order number. The PO number is how their system matches your invoice to money they already approved.
  5. What you did. A sentence describing the work, the dates it covered, and line items with a quantity, a unit, a rate, and an amount.
  6. The math, shown. Subtotal, discount, taxable subtotal, the sales tax rate and the tax, freight, total, deposits already paid, and the balance due. Whether tax applies to labor, materials, or freight depends on your state; the tool taxes the lines you mark and leaves freight untaxed.
  7. Progress billing. When this is one of several invoices on a contract: the contract or PO amount, which invoice this is (2 of 3), billed to date, and what remains. Public agencies and general contractors expect it.
  8. How to pay. Who to make the check out to, where to send it, ACH or card options, and any late fee or early-payment discount.
  9. A thank-you and a contact. One line, and the name of the person who can answer a question about the invoice.

This is general information from a software company, not tax or legal advice. Your accountant knows your state's rules.
