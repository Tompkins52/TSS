Updates

What has changed, newest first.

Company news and product releases in one list. Each product page carries its own changelog; this is the summary.

  1. November 7, 2026

    New logos for Upfront and Billhead, and Pass Muster Training

    Upfront for Small Business has its own mark: an arrow sweeping up out of a red and white shop awning. Each Upfront app's mark carries the same awning, starting with Billhead, whose invoice sheet now wears it across the top, and each one opens the same way: its own mark and name, then "by Upfront for Small Business". Upfront's pricing rule is now plain prices: no hidden fees, no contract, and you can cancel anytime. Billhead keeps its own promise of no subscription. Squared Away Training is now Pass Muster Training: the same study apps, starting with Part 107 Prep, and the old address forwards to the new one.

  2. November 7, 2026

    Our products in families, and customer products on their own page

    We do two kinds of work, and the site now says so from the first screen: we make our own apps, and we build software for partners under their name. Our own apps come in families, each with its own name, mark, and rules, and more families will follow. Upfront for Small Business is for running a small business, with plain prices, no ads, and never a cut of your money. Billhead is the first, and fleet tracking and timesheets are planned. Pass Muster Training is for studying toward a license, a certification, or an entrance exam, a few minutes a day, with what you miss coming back until it sticks. Part 107 Prep is the first, and PMP, CDL, and ASVAB prep are planned. IntelliSafe, built for IntelliSafe Systems LLC, and Phantom Soldier, built pro bono for Project Veteran Overwatch, are now under Customer products.

  3. November 7, 2026

    Billhead: under $100 is free, and one tap sends the invoice

    Any invoice under $100 is now free, whichever kind, as many as you like; the other prices stay as they were, and everything stays free during launch. Send to customer opens your email app with the PDF attached and the customer's address, the subject, and a short message filled in, so sending an invoice is a tap and then Send. Home opens on a ledger card with what is outstanding, overdue, and paid this month. An Android app is on its way to Google Play; it keeps your invoices in the app's own folder, which Android backs up and carries to a new phone.

  4. November 4, 2026

    Witch Card in sixty seconds

    A one-minute video on the Witch Card page: Dave has five credit cards and no idea what any of them do, the bank's forty-seven page booklet levels his kitchen table, his money app offers him a sixth card, and then the app does its thing at the register. The last twenty seconds are the real app, nothing drawn.

  5. November 3, 2026

    Billhead opens, and takes over from TSS Invoice Creator

    Billhead makes invoices from your phone. Set up your company once, then make a simple or an advanced invoice by answering a few questions or by typing on the invoice itself. Projects billed in stages say which invoice this is, what was billed before, and what remains; payments turn invoices partly paid and paid; and the PDF goes out locked, with an Invoice ID on every page. Nothing leaves your device. It is free while in testing, opens from tompkinssoftwaresystems.com/billhead, and replaces the free Invoice Creator that was on this site; an invoice saved in the old tool moves across in one tap.

  6. November 1, 2026

    Witch Card gets a new mascot, and Compare tells the truth about travel

    The home-screen icon and the welcome note have a new cartoon mascot, a card in a witch hat flying its broom across the moon. Compare now counts flights and hotels as travel, so a premium travel card is no longer judged on its base rate, and when one of your cards pays extra at one company, like 5x at Lyft, the row says so with an asterisk. Adding a card lists the best-known cards first and business cards last, and a card with rotating categories shows this quarter's categories and whether they are activated wherever it sits in your wallet.

  7. October 31, 2026

    Witch Card: one screen at the counter, Compare rebuilt, and a camera that reads your card

    The card to use and its rate come first with nothing to type, and the rest of your wallet fills the screen under it, ranked. Compare is now a scale from weak to great for each kind of place, with sign-up offers ranked for your goal and a row of banks to pick from; a card you already hold that ties for the top is listed first, so it never suggests a card you do not need. Hold a card up to the camera and the app reads its name on the phone, letters only, never the number. The original Chase Freedom joins the list. More motion throughout, and fine curvy lines drift behind everything.

  8. October 31, 2026

    Witch Card finds the store faster and asks when it is not sure

    A second map source answers in under a second when the others are busy. The app only says you are at a store when it is sure, offers the stores near you otherwise, guesses what kind of place an unknown name is, and remembers a spot you name so it knows next time. The Pay screen is lighter: the kinds of place are compact pills, and the answer fits on one screen.

  9. October 29, 2026

    Which Card is now Witch Card

    Same app, new name. The mascot is a card dressed as a witch, a witch hat lands on the card to use, and the home-screen icon is a card in a witch hat. It opens from tompkinssoftwaresystems.com/witch-card, and the old link still works. No magic, just math: every answer is still arithmetic on your own cards.

  10. October 29, 2026

    Which Card gets a new look

    The card to use is now drawn big, as if just pulled from your wallet, with the rest of your cards ranked in a pocket under it. The store you are standing in also shows up faster, and right away at places you have been in the last 30 days.

  11. October 28, 2026

    Which Card opens as a phone web app

    A phone app that tells you which credit card to use at the register: open it at the store and it tells you where you are standing and which of your cards earns the most there, with the rest ranked under it. It starts with 228 cards from 25 issuers, a perks checklist with links to each bank's activation page, and the best card you do not have at each store. It works in any phone browser at tompkinssoftwaresystems.com/which-card, and the App Store and Google Play versions are in testing.

  12. October 23, 2026

    Public Works Safety Training is now IntelliSafe

    The platform takes the IntelliSafe name and is licensed by IntelliSafe Systems LLC, with Tompkins Software Systems as its developer. The same release adds seven modules for nineteen in all, a new hire track, individual assignments, supervisor copies of training reminders, an eight-section incident report with diagrams and signed statements, construction and general industry inspection checklists, an annual safety program audit, and Excel export.

  13. October 21, 2026

    Tompkins Software Systems LLC organized in Illinois

    The Illinois Secretary of State files the Articles of Organization and the company exists. It takes over publishing and support for the products that had been built independently, and will hold the organization accounts on the Apple App Store and Google Play.

  14. October 17, 2026

    Company website published at tompkinssoftwaresystems.com

    Products, policies, and procurement information in one place. Contact channels and the newsletter open shortly.

  15. October 15, 2026

    Part 107 Prep 1.1: the mobile app, with daily goals and spaced review

    The iOS and Android app arrives with all progress kept on the phone, then the same day gains a daily goal, a forgiving streak with two automatic freezes, spaced review of missed questions, a question of the day, exam resume, and sixteen achievements. Store submission is next.

  16. October 15, 2026

    Phantom Soldier: I Can't See the Glass is out

    The new single's page goes live on phantomsoldier.org with players, release links, and the drawing it started from, plus a printable flyer with QR codes and tappable links in the PDF.

  17. October 14, 2026

    Painless Project Management public demo opens

    The password gate comes off the demo build. Anyone can open it, load the three example projects, and run their own.

  18. October 12, 2026

    Phantom Soldier site rebuilt as pages, with a VSO finder

    One long page becomes a home overview plus Music, Stories, How it works, Tell your story, Resources, and About. The Resources page gains a Veterans Service Officer finder by ZIP code, built from the VA's accreditation list and refreshed monthly by a scheduled job.

  19. October 8, 2026

    Public Works Safety Training browser edition

    The twelve modules, quizzes, certificates, and incident reporting now run in any browser with nothing to install, with one-click hosting options for the full platform.

  20. October 7, 2026

    Public Works Safety Training platform released

    Annual OSHA training with a ten-question quiz per module, certificates, automatic yearly reassignment, email reminders, incident reporting with an investigation workflow, and compliance reports, deployable with Docker or on a Linux or Windows server.

  21. October 6, 2026

    Part 107 Prep study site launched

    Five study modules, the certification process step by step, 14 CFR Part 107 section by section, a printable preflight checklist, and a practice exam engine with more than 200 original questions.

  22. September 30, 2026

    Painless Project Management 1.0

    The one-page method for running a project: charter it, run it, report it, with CSV export to monday.com or any other board.

  23. September 30, 2026

    Phantom Soldier: Hard to Break single and site redesign

    A second song, embedded players, song pages, and the first print pieces: a tear-off flyer and an 11 by 17 poster with QR codes.

  24. August 22, 2026

    phantomsoldier.org goes live on its own domain

    The first version of the site for Project Veteran Overwatch, with the first song, Still Here.
