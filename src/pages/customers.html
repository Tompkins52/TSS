Nonprofits

We set aside a share of our time every year for pro bono work, and we start with nonprofits that serve veterans. Other nonprofits can work with us at reduced rates.

The pro bono program

  • The organization owns everything. The code, the design, the app store listings, the accounts. If you leave, nothing of yours stays with us.
  • Same process as paid work. A one-page charter, a weekly update, and an after-action review. Free does not mean casual.
  • Scoped to finish. We take on work we can complete, not work we can start. One or two engagements a year.
  • No strings. No logo placement requirement, no case study requirement, no upsell.

Our first pro bono commitment is to Project Veteran Overwatch, the organization behind Phantom Soldier, where veterans' stories become songs. We built and maintain its website at no charge. PVO owns what it publishes; we only build.

Your idea, built and licensed

Some of the best software ideas come from people who know a field inside out and have never written a line of code: the inspector who knows exactly what the form should ask, the coach with a better way to run a season, the hobbyist who has mapped every gap in the tools they use. We help those people build the platform and own it.

How it works

  1. Scope it. We turn the idea into a one-page charter: what it does, who it is for, what the first version includes, what it costs, and when.
  2. Build it. We build the platform and you review it as it takes shape, with a two-minute written update every week.
  3. Publish it. Web, Apple App Store, Google Play, or all three, under your name or ours, whichever the charter says.
  4. License it. We set up the licensing so you can sell it: per-seat, per-organization, or a one-time purchase, with the terms written down and the money going where it should.

What you keep

The idea, the name, and the rights, as written in the charter. Our fee is a fixed price for the build, or a share of license revenue if you would rather not pay up front. Either way, the arrangement is in writing before the first line of code.

Bring us the idea

Three paragraphs are enough: what it does, who would pay for it, and what you have tried so far. We reply to every one and we say no honestly when we cannot finish it.

How to buy

We are a small company and we know what a public procurement file needs. Here is how an engagement works and what we can provide for your paperwork.

Products

The published products are free to use as described on their pages. Hosted setup, content customization, and support agreements are quoted per organization. Quotes are written, itemized, and valid for 60 days.

Custom builds

  1. Conversation. Thirty minutes about the problem. No slides.
  2. Charter. One page: what we are building, why, what done looks like, what it costs, and when. You approve it before anything is built.
  3. Build. A two-minute written update every week: schedule, budget, and whether we need something from you.
  4. Delivery and after-action review. The software, the documentation, the source code if the charter says so, and a written review of what we would do differently.

What we accept

  • Purchase orders from government and nonprofit customers, net 30
  • Card or invoice for small businesses and individuals, half at the charter and half at delivery
  • Fixed-price contracts for custom builds; time and materials only by request
  • Your vendor forms, insurance questionnaires, and security questionnaires
