What it is

We build software with a mix of automated coding, research, and review tools alongside our own work. Left to themselves, those tools drift: they overrun budgets, grade their own homework, and leave no record of what was planned versus what happened. TOC is the discipline layer. It is how a one-person company ships four products with a paper trail.

What it does

  • Charter gateObjective, business case, definition of done, budget, and dates. Nothing gets planned until the charter passes. Same rule as Painless Project Management.
  • Plan as a task graphA pipeline template expands the charter into tasks with dependencies, a critical path, and parallel lanes, with warnings when the plan overruns the charter's dates.
  • RoutingEach kind of task (research, plan, write, code, test, review) goes to the tool best suited to it, with fallbacks.
  • No self-gradingA review task is never run by the tool that produced the work. If only the author is available, the review is blocked rather than faked.
  • Budget capSpend is checked between tasks and passed down as a per-task limit, so one runaway task cannot blow the budget.
  • Run log and roll-upEvery attempt, cost, and duration is logged. One status line per project shows schedule, spend against budget, and who needs help.
  • After-action reportWhat was planned, what happened, why, what to sustain, what to improve. Lessons feed back into the next plan.

Availability

TOC runs on our own machines and is not offered for sale. If the method interests your organization, the same ideas, minus the automation, are in Painless Project Management.
