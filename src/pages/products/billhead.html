    What it does

    Most small businesses invoice the same way every time: the same company details, the same terms, often the same customers and the same kinds of work. Billhead remembers all of it, so an invoice is the customer, the work, and the price. Jobs that are billed in stages get the arithmetic most apps leave to you.

    • Your company, set up onceYour name or your logo, address, terms, tax rate, invoice numbering, and how to pay you, with a payment link if you have one. Every new invoice starts filled in, customers are saved the first time you bill them, and the lines you have billed before are a tap away.
    • Simple or advancedA simple invoice is who, what, how much, sales tax, a discount or a deposit, when it is due, and how to pay. An advanced invoice adds a PO and a reference, the job site, the service period, units and tax by line, freight, retainage, late fee terms, and your signature.
    • Ask me, or type on the pageAsk me walks through one question at a time and ends on the finished invoice. On the page, the invoice is the form: tap any part of it and type, and dashed links add the parts you need. Switch between the two whenever you like.
    • Jobs billed in stagesA project holds the contract amount, approved change orders, the PO, and retainage. Each new invoice on it says which one it is (2 of 3), what was billed before, and what remains, and the project shows billed, paid, outstanding, and retainage held. Several projects can run at once.
    • Sent, overdue, paidDownloading an invoice marks it sent. It turns overdue on its due date, partly paid or paid as you record payments by check, ACH, card, or anything else, and a paid invoice gets a PAID stamp. Home shows what is outstanding and what needs attention.
    • A PDF that stays as you sent itDrawn on your phone in clean type with your logo, signed in the app if you choose, and locked so viewers will not let it be edited. Every page carries an Invoice ID made from what the invoice says, so a copy that comes back can be checked against the one you sent.
    What is in each invoice, the lock, pricing, and where your invoices live

    Simple and advanced

    Simple: your name or logo and details, the customer, an invoice number, the date, terms, and the due date, line items with a quantity and a rate, one sales tax, a discount, a deposit received, payment instructions with a pay link, and a note. One page.

    Advanced, everything in simple and: the customer's PO and your reference, an attention line, the job site or ship-to address, the service period, a units column, tax by line, freight, retainage, late fee or early-payment terms, progress billing on a project (contract amount, previously billed, this invoice, billed to date, remaining), your drawn signature, a line for the customer to sign, your tax ID or a VAT or GST number, another currency, and terms and conditions. A simple invoice can switch to advanced at any time; adding an advanced part asks first.

    Sending it

    Send to customer opens your email app with the PDF attached and the customer's address, the subject, and a short message already filled in: the invoice number, the amount due, the due date, the PO, how to pay. You press Send, and it goes from your own address; nothing passes through us. On a phone, Share PDF puts the invoice into Messages, Drive, or any other app, and Download or Save to phone keeps a copy. Each send is noted on the invoice.

    The lock and the signature

    The PDF opens in any viewer without a password, prints, and lets its text be copied, and it carries a permission that tells viewers not to allow edits. Acrobat, Preview, and the major browsers honor it. Software made to strip PDF permissions can still remove it, which is why every page also carries the Invoice ID: if a copy comes back with different numbers, the ID tells you which version you actually sent. A drawn signature prints above a line with your name, your title, and the date it was signed in the app.

    Pricing, plainly

    Any invoice under $100 is free, whichever kind, forever. Above that, your first invoice is free; then 99 cents for a simple invoice and $1.99 for an advanced one, paid once when you download or send it. Packs of 10 simple ($7.99), 10 advanced ($14.99), or 50 advanced ($49) never expire, and a year of unlimited invoices is $99, paid once, with no auto-renew. Editing or sending again an invoice you have paid for is free. There is no account to keep and nothing renews on its own. During launch, every invoice is free.

    Where your invoices live

    On your phone or computer, in the browser's own storage. There is no account and no server. A backup file under More holds your company, customers, projects, and invoices; keep one somewhere safe and use it to move to a new phone. More also saves every invoice as a CSV file for your accountant.

    Coming from TSS Invoice Creator

    Billhead replaces the free invoice tool that used to be on this site. If you have an invoice saved in it, open the old tool's address on the same device and tap Move it to Billhead: your business details and the invoice come across in one step.

    Put it on your home screen

    On an iPhone, open it in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. On Android, open it in Chrome, tap the menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app. It opens full screen like any other app and works without a signal once it has loaded. On an iPhone it also keeps your invoices safer: Safari clears a website's saved data after seven days without a visit, while an app on the home screen keeps its own count, which starts over every time you open it.
