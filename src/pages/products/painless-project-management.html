What it does

Most projects in a department are run by someone whose actual job is something else. They need to know what was approved, where the money stands, whether the next milestone is on time, and who to ask for help. That is the whole product.

  • 1. Charter itOne page: department, sponsor, lead, why, what, what we get, approved budget, whether it needs a capital request or a contract, start and end dates. Until the page is complete, it is not a project.
  • 2. Run itUp to eight milestones. Three money numbers: approved, spent, forecast. A two-minute weekly update: schedule, budget, and whether you need help, and where.
  • 3. Report itEvery project rolls up to one view sorted by who needs attention: help requests, off-track schedule or budget, and projects that have gone quiet. The executive summary writes itself.
  • Export anywhereThe roll-up exports to CSV with one row per project and one column per field, flat on purpose so it maps directly to monday.com or any other board.
  • Rules of the roadEvery project has a sponsor and a lead; nobody else needs a title. Eight milestones, maximum. Post the update on Friday even when nothing changed. Red is not failure; hiding red is.
  • Single user now, team laterThe public demo keeps projects in your browser. The same page runs against a shared database for a team; every read and write goes through one small storage object, so that is a configuration change, not a rewrite.
