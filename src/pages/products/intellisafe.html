The problem it solves

Public works crews have to complete the same OSHA training every year, and someone has to prove it. In most departments that proof is a spreadsheet, a binder of sign-in sheets, and a supervisor who remembers to ask. Incident reports go in a drawer, witness statements sit on loose paper, inspections live on clipboards, and the investigation, if it happens, is not written down anywhere a future auditor can find it.

IntelliSafe puts training, quizzes, certificates, reminders, incident investigations, and inspections in one place that a small department can actually run, either in a browser with nothing to install or on a server the department controls.

What it does

  • Nineteen training modulesOSHA's ten most frequently cited standards; pole top and bucket truck rescue for electric and public services crews; bloodborne pathogens, fire safety and extinguishers, electrical safety, confined spaces, ergonomics, and workplace violence with active shooter response; and incident reporting and investigation for supervisors and managers.
  • A quiz for every moduleTen questions, 80 percent to pass (configurable), retakes allowed, every attempt recorded, and a printable certificate on completion.
  • Assignments that keep themselves currentWhen an employee passes, next year's assignment is created automatically. Ten modules form a required new hire track, assigned in one step when an employee is added, and the safety manager can assign any module to any employee.
  • Reminders that reach supervisionEmail thirty days before training is due, again at seven days, and every seven days once it is overdue, with a copy to the employee's supervisor each time. Supervisors are also told when their people finish a module.
  • An eight-section incident reportIncident information, vehicle and equipment, personal injury, description, principal and eyewitness statements, facts and analysis, and a signature block for the investigator, manager, safety manager, and department assistant director. Report numbers carry the date, the type, and the count for that day, such as 10232026PI-01.
  • Diagrams instead of paragraphsCircle the damage on a vehicle diagram, draw the scene over an intersection sketch, and mark injuries on a body diagram, with checklists for injury type and body part.
  • Statements signed by the people who gave themPick principal or eyewitness from a list and add as many as the incident needs. Each person sees only their own statement page, then signs and dates it on the investigator's device, through an emailed link, or on paper.
  • An investigation that has to be finishedAnalysis of the facts, contributing causes, direct causes, root cause, and the contributing factor type: behavioral, engineered, or environmental. Corrective actions carry a responsible party, a target date, and a verification date, with the policies and regulations that apply. A report cannot be closed until the causes are recorded.
  • Site inspections and an annual auditChecklists for construction work (136 items) and for general industry facilities and shops (137 items), plus a 69-item annual safety program audit. Each item cites its OSHA reference, and anything that needs action becomes a finding with a responsible party and a target date.
  • Records that hold upThe full incident report prints to paper or PDF with its diagrams, statements, and signatures. Training records and inspections export to Excel. In the full platform, employees see what they reported, supervisors see their division and team, and safety administrators see everything.

Keeping the content current

The module list follows OSHA's annual list of most frequently cited standards. The ranking lives in one data file and is updated once a year. Each module and each inspection checklist is a plain text file an administrator can edit and reload without a developer.
